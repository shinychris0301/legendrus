# Gameplay R4 — readability and effects review

The user requested an opinion on match effects, generated boosters, small gem fragments, indistinct/unfinished obstacles and upper HUD readability/layout. This is a review and recommendation, not authorization inferred for a new art implementation. Gameplay remains revision 4; the existing score-frame correction and earlier page decisions are preserved.

The generated board specials and their animation need a consistent design family. The footer inventory medallions are a separate system and can remain. Normal gems already provide the strongest material/color reference.

| Area | Finding | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Small gems beside specials | Match3Board creates a 27×29 SpecialMatchColor image at the bottom-right of each special. These are persistent match-identity markers, not emitted debris. They look detached in the retained special-piece capture. | Integrate the underlying gem or shield identity into the special's main setting/socket. Preserve color AND silhouette distinctions: sapphire and shield are separate tile identities despite both being blue. Do not simply delete the marker without a replacement. |
| Board-generated specials | The row special is a blue banded crystal; the column special is a red-tipped blue rocket. Burst and rainbow pieces add two more different treatments. | Establish a matched horizontal/vertical family, a clearly distinct area-burst piece and a recognizable color-clear piece. Use restrained antique-gold housings, readable gem cores and similar scale/lighting. Review normal, created, selected, activated and removed states together. |
| Matching/booster effects | PooledUiVfxLayer builds Images without assigned sprites. Match waves call EmitBursts, which emits expanding board rectangles because its motion data lacks actual cell lists. Retained Firebird activation shows a broad translucent wash. | Localized crystal glints and a few shaped shards for normal matches; clear formation animation for new specials; directional trails, feather/flame, selected-color rays and blue pearl motion for corresponding actions. Connect effects to real affected positions, preserve pool/input limits, and inspect motion rather than only idle screenshots. |
| Obstacles | Ice uses one image at 0.48 opacity, blending detailed frost with equally detailed gem facets. Ice/chain reuse the same art across layers; reinforced crates share art for multiple remaining-layer values. Numeric layer labels carry distinctions the artwork does not show. | Make ice edges/cracks readable with a clear center, enlarge chain links and separate wood/metal silhouettes. Provide explicit intact/damaged/break states for supported ice, chain and crate mechanics. Higher resolution alone will not fix overlapping fine detail and low opacity. Stone/web remain unsupported mechanics, not merely missing sprites. |
| Upper text and placement | Name/level use light serif treatment at effective reference sizes 32/23; level becomes about 14 pixels in the 576-wide capture. Their group sits high beside the portrait. The renderer uses doubled glyph size at half transform scale with a gold gradient/shadow. | Lower/rebalance the name, level and progress group; increase small-label size/weight and simplify edge effects. Verify at actual display size before changing the font-rendering path. Half-scale glyph generation is not by itself proof of a defect; downscaled HTML captures can add softness. No native/device blur diagnosis is claimed from this inspection alone. |
| Stars | 44×46 reference bounds and thin dark-centered inactive outlines become weak at narrow sizes. Stars currently represent score thresholds, not simply round completion. | Larger, readable silhouettes, stronger inactive rims and clearly filled earned stars; separate them from nearby filigree and give the progress bar a clearer track. Preserve real thresholds. |
| Goals | Supplied gem icons exist; score/survival now use basic geometric star/hourglass glyphs in ornate map-node frames. These do not share the painterly material finish of the other assets. Small counts and busy decoration weaken reading. | Finish a consistent goal icon family, with recognizable obstacle/score/survival/feather symbols, larger solid counts, aligned baselines and clear complete states. Match each goal to the actual board object. |

Recommended scope: one coordinated Gameplay readability-and-effects revision. Settle the whole upper HUD composition and the special/obstacle state designs first. Implement in reviewable passes: upper HUD clarity (always visible), then special-piece generation/matching/activation and obstacle damage effects. Keep the normal gem art, footer, existing rules and earlier pages. Do not call the page finished based on idle captures alone; later verification should include short action sequences at phone scale.
